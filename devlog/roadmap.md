## Upcoming Milestones

### M3 - Manual Dispatch, Autonomous Execution, Manual Review

**Objective:** Move from interactive prompting to structured single-task execution with enough logging to verify the agent is doing useful work. Requires the two-layer foundation from M2.

**Depends on:** the M2 two-layer foundation, closed in [`changelog.md`](changelog.md#m2---reasoningcapability-layer-separation) (headless mode requires the capability layer tool interface; task briefs are the operator input channel from M1.5).

**Finding -- Sub-milestone containment (recorded, not designed):** Milestones and sub-milestones are intended to be self-contained, but partial implementations from later milestones are frequently needed while the current milestone is incomplete. This suggests that how features are cut into sub-milestones, and how strictly they are sequenced, may be the wrong seam. The sub-milestone-as-container model is recognized as a candidate for re-examination, not as settled. Design and any restructuring is deferred to M3. The current deferred-items / sub-milestone task-list system is maintained until then.

**Sub-milestone order (operator direction, 2026-10-25).** The four milestones that share the maintenance and instruction surfaces are ordered M3.2.2, then M3.3.3, then M3.3.5, then M3.5. The order runs the instruments before the record they check: M3.2.2 settles what a maintenance and review payload is, M3.3.3 settles the convention corpus those payloads land in, M3.3.5 measures whether the settled surfaces change behaviour, and M3.5 rewrites the task record last, because its invariant census retires a judgement pass from `roadmap-maintenance` and its parallel-milestone rule amends the `task-list` convention, both of which the earlier three edit. M3.1.2 rides alongside M3.2.2, because it owns the gates that consolidation's deletions must pass. Each of those milestones' `**Sequencing:**` line points here instead of restating the order.

- [x] **The resilient model router in the reasoning layer** -- a pi extension (`model-failover`, under [`src/reasoning/providers/pi/config/agent/extensions/`](../src/reasoning/providers/pi/config/agent/extensions/model-failover/)) that keeps a session on a working model. The operator declares chains in `routes.json`; each chain becomes a virtual model, and the extension dispatches the first healthy route, cools a route on the declared consecutive transient failures or the first quota-class failure, restores it on the next success, and degrades to the chain head when every route cools. The design record is the extension README, the measured behaviour and the state machine are recorded there, and the suite holds the machine with a mutation gate. It resolves the pending decision handover `20261012-01` left open, taking the switch-models option and generalizing it. Under a route it pins each inference request to a clean edge address of the provider's host, resolving the address itself and tracking the edge health; `x-opencode-log-id` is dropped because the edge identity is known by construction. Handover `20261014-14`.

#### M3.3 - Instruction Surface

The three instruction surfaces an agent reads -- prompts, skills and the two `AGENTS.md` layers -- and the rules that govern how one reaches the next. ##### M3.6.6 - The scheduler

**Objective:** hold the class of work that needs both a seat count and a lifecycle, so that neither dispatch nor the task queue has to grow into the other to serve it.

**Depends on:** nothing it blocks. It is filed unscoped so that a boundary decision elsewhere can point at it instead of proposing an extension of either mechanism.

**Sequencing:** none. It opens when an iteration is scoped against it, and until then every mechanism states its own boundary and stops there.

The seam is measured, not assumed. Dispatch owns role resolution, the runtime decision, the route memory, the seat count, the turn boundary and the failure rule. The task queue owns worktrees, branches, break points, verification and write-back, and its worker protocol is plain files, so a worker of any kind can take part even though its fork path spawns through pi. One brief at a time is dispatch's, and one fan-out under a human is the queue's. What neither owns is the shape between them: many briefs in one run, seats that span runtimes, a failure that changes what the next turn may use, and results that must land as one verified change set. A scheduler would subsume both. That is a boundary this milestone records rather than cuts.

- [ ] **The scheduler subsumes dispatch and the task queue** - unscoped on purpose. Known: it owns the seat count, the turn boundary, the failure rule, the worktree lifecycle and the write-back as one contract; it serves every runtime the table names rather than the workers pi spawns; and it keeps the single-writer property the turn boundary buys today.
