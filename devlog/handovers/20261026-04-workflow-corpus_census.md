# Handover - The corpus census

## Objective

Rule on every review-instruction carrier in one pass, and deliver the fate table that the consolidation acts on and the eval grades against, so no later step has to re-derive what a file's rules are worth.

## Scope

In scope: six surfaces in `.agents/audits/` and the two cleanup prompts, each carrier given a fate by subject cluster.

- The six surfaces carry 607 lines between them: `kelsey-code-reviewer` (158, bash and container), `architecture-doc-reviewer` (166, prose and docs), `dhh-code-audit` (99, Ruby and JavaScript), `bash-audit` (29, already pointer form), `test-quality-campaign` (106), `test-assertion-sweep-brief` (49, report-only).
- The two prompts under comparison are carriers too, and screening them is what makes the intervention hypothesis testable.
- `documentation-audit-comparison` and `surface-area-report` are out: settled records rather than instructions. The reason is stated here rather than carried as a task.
- Out of scope: the eval case set and any persona. Each has its own roadmap row; a census that edited files would be doing their work with worse evidence.
- **Amended mid-iteration, by operator ruling:** the consolidation is in scope for the bash carrier only. The census found that every rule `bash-audit` carried was already owned by the conventions document, and that seven rules `kelsey` stated had no owner at all, so the mechanical half of the consolidation could not be separated from the census of that carrier without losing the evidence the census existed to produce. The container, prose and unowned tail stay out and stay open on their roadmap row. Deletion of the carriers stays out too, which the last acceptance criterion states as a prohibition.
