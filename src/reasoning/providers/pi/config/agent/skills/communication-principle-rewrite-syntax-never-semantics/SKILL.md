# Syntax is the agent's; semantics is the operator's

**The pattern.**

| The agent may | The agent may not |
|---|---|
| join, reorder, restructure, organize the contents and add headings, cut a restatement, replace a definite or indefinite article | change scope, strength, actor, condition or exception; change a quantifier, a negation or a logical connective; substitute or drop a keyword; merge two instructions into one; resolve an ambiguity |

**Why.** The goal is to avoid drift from accepting unreviewed rewrites. Where a wording already leads to a form of evaluation we like, carry it unchanged: a gratuitous reflow is likely to break that evaluation.

**Disagreement.** A rewrite is not the place to correct the author. When the rewriter judges that a clause is wrong, unclear or suboptimal, the judgement is raised to the author rather than applied: state the clause, what it appears to require, the alternative the rewriter would prefer as a suggestion under the raise, and wait for the author's ruling. A keyword believed to name the wrong concept, a condition that looks over-broad, a misspelling inside a protected word, and an ambiguity the rewriter can resolve but the author has not are each this case.

**Fires on.** A rewrite of instruction text, by the agent rather than by its author, where the meaning must survive.

**What it does not cover.** Whether the rewritten text reads well: the other principles in this corpus. Whether the text belongs where it sits: `principle-own-the-scope-you-declare`.

The agent may rearrange, join, reorder, restructure, and organize the contents and add headings as general descriptors of the instruction, because none of that moves what the instruction requires. It may not touch the words that decide what is required: the quantifiers, the negations, the modals, the logical connectives, the actors, the conditions, the exceptions, and the keywords the instruction is anchored on.

**The test.** Read a finished instruction as a literal reader: if the words allow the reader to infer a task, an action or a conclusion the writer did not intend, one of the judgements in this corpus failed, and that failure is a disagreement: the clause is raised to the author, with the rephrase offered as a suggestion under the raise.

1. Would a reader do something different if they obeyed this instead? If yes, the change is the author's decision.
2. Has a quantifier, a negation, a modal or a logical connective changed its strength or its scope? If yes, the change is the author's decision, even if the sentence still reads correctly.
3. Do the keywords survive verbatim? A substituted term is the author's decision, because the term is the concept.
