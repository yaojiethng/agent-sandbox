# Align the mental model

Use one word for each thing and one meaning for each word, so the reader builds the model the writer holds.

**The pattern.** List the things the text names and the words it uses for them. Compare the two lists. A word holding two things, or a thing held by two words, is where the reader's model can diverge from the writer's.

| Instead of | Give |
|---|---|
| one word carrying two meanings | a distinct term for each, defined where they first differ |
| two words for one thing, swapped between sections | one term, used throughout |
| a word whose meaning the reader supplies | the definition before the first use |
| a term that changes between a summary and the section it summarises | the same term in both |
| a bucket of near-equivalents ("rule", "norm", "policy") for one thing | the one the repository defines |

**Why.** The reader builds a model from the words in front of them. When a word carries two meanings, the reader chooses one, and the model follows that choice. The text reports nothing. The writer meets the divergence at a review, or in work built on the other reading, and the sentence still reads correctly to them. A task's scope and its acceptance criteria are the same mapping, and two readers who map them differently have the same fault.

**The test.** Ask of each word whether a reader could place it on a second thing, and of each thing whether the text ever names it with a second word. A yes answer means two models are available.

A term the reader may not hold is defined at first use, per `communication-principle-define-terminology-before-use` at `$AGENT_HOME/agent/skills/communication-principle-define-terminology-before-use/SKILL.md`, which decides when a term needs defining, where this skill decides the mapping.
