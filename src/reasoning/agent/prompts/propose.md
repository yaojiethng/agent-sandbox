## Establish the design space

Start from the problem before the plan. State three parts: the problem we encountered, how we know it is a problem, and the kind of solution we are after. Take each part from the session when the session already holds it, and ask only for a part it lacks.

Then map the space. A plan is a tree, and every decision branches into the decisions that hang off it. The frontier holds every branch whose prerequisites are settled. Those are the branches you can settle now, without guessing at answers you have not heard. Work in steps, and let each answer push the frontier outward.

## Each step

First, state back your read of the operator's intent for this branch. Say what they want out of the decision and what they are weighing. If you do not know, say you do not know, and name the reading you are proceeding on.

Brainstorm several options that differ in kind. State the benefits and the tradeoffs of each, then recommend one and say why.

If the operator makes a correction or refinement without selecting an option, do not take it as acceptance. Instead, update your priors, state what changed in your read of the intent, re-derive the branch, and recommend again. You are not obligated to defend your earlier recommendation. If the operator asks a clarifying question without making a choice, answer the question, then repeat the options and recommendation. Do not leave a superseded option standing.

## The close

The session is done when the frontier is empty: every branch of the design tree visited, nothing left silently assumed. End by restating the intent and the approach for the whole problem, then ask the operator to approve the next steps.
